    § 5 The Instruments

    The painted echoes are live radar — the last two hours and a half-hour nowcast, draped over the terrain itself. The dial beneath the map sets the whole day out as a ruler — night in ink, the sun's gold arc between its true rising and setting — and the blue window upon it is the loop itself: play it or slide through time. Doppler velocity paints the wind the radar itself measures, from the nearest Weather Service station — green air moving toward the radar, red away; a tight green-red couplet is rotation (best read with Radar switched off, United States only). Switch on Cloud tops to loft the latest infrared satellite view into a three-dimensional cloud deck — the taller a column stands, the colder and higher its top. Rain, last 24 h raises a column from the ground wherever rain has fallen since this hour yesterday — the taller and deeper its color, the more water came down. Wind flow sets the air itself moving as drifting trails, and Alerts outlines active National Weather Service warnings (United States) in their conventional colors — tap one for its particulars. Any warnings for the chosen place appear beneath its name. Hurricane tracking draws active tropical cyclones from the National Hurricane Center — the forecast cone, the storm's past and projected track, and each forecast position (tap one for its time, winds and category). Active storms are listed below the toggles.

    The buttons at the upper right hold a pencil — switch it on to sketch freehand over the map in red, anchored to the land so your marks ride the terrain as you pan and zoom; the bin beside it erases them. The downward arrow saves the present view as a JPG.
